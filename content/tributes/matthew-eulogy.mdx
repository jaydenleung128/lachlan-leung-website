No matter how quietly I poked my head into his room, Lockie already knew why I was there. Before I could say a word, he had already logged in.
Already sent the invite request.
Already telling me which character to pick because of "good team synergy".

When the game started, he'd go first out and lead the attacks.
I'd stay behind him, anchoring as support.
That was our rhythm, and it always worked.

But that wasn't just how he played games.
He was always out front.
Always leading.
Always pushing forward.

He trained 6 days a week and dedicated every summer to a training camp.
He has a cabinet full of state and national medals.
And at just 16 years old, he was ranked 32nd in the country.

There was never a challenge too big for him.
No ceiling he quietly accepted.
No limit he stayed under.

I coach younger kids every Wednesday at Peter Masen's table tennis club.
And without fail, someone will always ask... "Where's Lockie?"
"Is he coming back soon?"
"I miss watching his long rallies with Chris Kwok."

Actually… this one time…
One of the kids asked, "Can I touch Lockie's drink bottle." They had recognised the one I borrowed from him while he was doing chemo.

Silly it may sound, but it wasn't about the bottle.
It was because it belonged to Lockie, as if it contained some magical essence of him.
The kids didn't just watch him play, they looked up to him.
They wanted to be closer to him, even when he wasn't in the room. They believed in themselves BECAUSE they saw him. They witnessed him raising the bar time and time again with passion and determination. And this mere bottle he owned, brought them this bit closer.

That's the kind of presence Lockie had. That's the kind of influence he had.

He never boasted.
But I think, quietly, he knew how magnetic he was. He was loved by everyone he crossed paths with — everyone here today is a testament to that.

Lockie was always thinking of people in small, quiet ways.
After school, he'd walk to the Padstow shops for his go-to Tiger Rolls at Tama Sushi.
He'd always ask me if I wanted something too. Same with Broaster chicken, always remembering the extra chicken salt.

And when he negotiated a DoorDash subscription from dad – and yes, he absolutely negotiated it –
He never ordered just for himself.
He always included me.

Lockie also loved to share his music with me.
Lockie loved K-Pop – ILLIT, NewJeans and LE SSERAFIM.
Not just casually – but properly.
He knew all the groups… and shared all the latest tea.

I used to think K-Pop was kind of lame – my taste hasn't changed in years, but somehow, he pulled me in too.
There's a playlist on his Spotify called KPOP x HARD – short for hardstyle.
I like to think he curated that for us.
His world and my world, blended into one.

I will miss you so much Lockie.
I will miss hearing you open the door.
I will miss hearing your thudding footsteps across the floorboards,
I will miss seeing you leaning against my doorframe with your phone in your hand ready to take my order.
I will miss the meals we were meant to share.
I will miss you every time I hear any of your favorite songs.

Lockie was also brave, and courageous.
He was never afraid of people.
Never afraid to ask or speak up.
And through him… I became less afraid too.

But the hardest thing to say is that I wish it were reversed —
…that Lockie was protected, from harm, from cancer.

**Instead… he protected us all**

During his treatment, he still played games with me, mum, Jayden, and the cousins.
He still went to school when he had the strength.
He still showed up every Tuesday at St George to cheer on his team.
He put his cancer aside so we all could be normal for just a moment. He showed up because loved his life and the people in it.

And when we were told his cancer was incurable,
He cried "I love my life".
He cried, "I was born into a good family".
He cried "I'm going to miss you all"

Yet even in all his pain, Lockie led us to each extended family to share the devastating news himself.
He called the meeting at school to tell his friends and teachers himself.
On the Korea and Hong Kong trip,
He never wallowed in his suffering and approached each day with energy and excitement.

Secretly, he kept a symptom diary which I read after… and later learnt how much pain he shielded from us.
Lockie carried it for us all so that we could enjoy one last adventure together.

No 17-year-old is expected to be this brave.
No 17-year-old is expected to be this courageous.
No 17-year-old is expected to hide their suffering to protect the family.
No 17-year-old is expected to show leadership under impossible circumstances.

But Lockie did.
Because that's who he was.

Lockie…
I don't know how I'm going to do this without you.
You showed me how to be brave.
You showed me how to go after what you want.
You showed me how to live life with purpose.
I will honor you every day that I live.

I love you, Lockie.
