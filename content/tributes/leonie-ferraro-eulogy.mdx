Good morning. My name is Leonie Ferraro, and I am the Deputy Principal of Lachlan's year group at Menai High School. I feel deeply humbled and honoured to have been asked to speak today about Lachlan on behalf of the staff and students of Menai High School.

It goes without saying that Lachlan was a much-loved and highly respected student within our school community. Looking back through his school reports, they are filled with words that consistently reflect his character and approach to learning: diligent, enthusiastic, talented, insightful, and demonstrating admirable determination. His classwork was often described as being of exceptional quality.

From Year 7 onwards, Lachlan continued to grow and improve, applying himself with quiet commitment and purpose. By Year 12, he was undertaking a rigorous and demanding pattern of study, including Extension 2 Mathematics, Physics, and Advanced English — a reflection of both his ability and his strong work ethic.

However, as we all know, school is about far more than academic success alone. Lachlan's parents often shared with me how much he genuinely loved school, and I believe what truly set Lachlan apart was the way he wholeheartedly embraced not only his learning, but also his friendships and his sense of citizenship.

In 2024, Lachlan was one of very few students in the history of MHS to complete the prestigious Diamond Award, alongside his good friend Caleb. This achievement reflects not only ability, but sustained commitment and character. To attain the Diamond Award, over a 20-week period, students are required to demonstrate excellence or leadership in academics, sport, or community involvement.

Lachlan met and truly embodied these criteria through his academic dedication, his sporting commitment, and, importantly, through his wholehearted commitment to Menai High School's exit outcome of Skilled Citizenship and our school motto of Care and Commitment.

Lachlan was my "go-to" student when new students joined our school partway through the year. He warmly welcomed them, took them on tours of the school, and made sure they felt included by inviting them into his friendship group. He was also recognised for his involvement in our Japanese Study Tour programs, generously giving his time and energy to ensure our visiting students felt welcome and had a meaningful and enjoyable experience.

However, beyond all of this, I believe the most meaningful email I have received in my entire career was one I received in May last year from the convener of the NSW Secondary School Boys Shield Table Tennis Championships. I am going to read it in its entirety, as every word captures the kind of person Lachlan was and the way he will be remembered.

> I wanted to take a moment to share some feedback following the recent table tennis tournament and, in particular, to acknowledge one of your students, Lachlan Leung, whose presence and actions were truly inspiring.
>
> Throughout the day, Lachlan displayed not only skill and composure on the table but also an extraordinary sense of generosity and leadership. He consistently went out of his way to support and encourage his teammates and even students from other schools. Whether it was offering quiet advice between matches, helping others refine their technique, or simply lifting the spirits of those feeling nervous or defeated, Lachlan made every player around him feel more confident and valued.
>
> What stood out most was his ability to bring out the best in others through quiet, genuine support and humility. It was clear that his primary goal wasn't to win, but to make the experience more meaningful for everyone involved. In doing so, he set a powerful example of sportsmanship, empathy, and maturity.
>
> Lachlan's behaviour throughout the tournament is a testament to the values we all strive to instil in students. He represented your school with pride, not just in his performance, but in his character. I believe his impact reached far beyond the table tennis arena, and many of the students who were there will carry that experience with them.
>
> Thank you for taking the time to read this. I just felt it was important to share what a remarkable young person Lachlan is.
>
> Kindest regards,
> Joshua Luc — Teacher PDHPE, Macquarie Fields High School

When Lachlan's parents informed me of his illness in January 2025, I worked closely with his medical care team to ensure that Lachlan could continue with his schooling alongside his treatment, with the goal of graduating with his year group in 2026. Together, we considered the very real challenges he faced — prolonged absences, treatment-related fatigue, chemo brain, and periods of illness.

When I shared these challenges with Lachlan's teachers, every single staff member responded with the same unwavering attitude — that as a school, we would do whatever it took to support Lachlan to complete his HSC in a way that reflected his true ability, not what his illness might suggest was possible. On numerous occasions, hospital staff commented that they wished other schools were as supportive as Menai High School. I would always respond that it wasn't the school, it was the student. Lachlan was the special one, and every one of his teachers wholeheartedly supported him because of who he was.

In December when Lachlan received the devastating news that his cancer was terminal, he bravely came to school to share this with me, his Year Advisers, Dennis our Student Support Officer, his friends, and his teachers. After sharing such difficult news, we were able to provide Lachlan and his friends with a classroom where they could do what they did every recess and lunch: sit together, talk, and play cards. A lively and joyful game of poker followed and carried on until the end of the school day.

As I walked with Lachlan to the office that afternoon, he turned to me and said, "This was the best afternoon, I even forgot I was sick." That was the last time I saw Lachlan, and possibly the last words he ever said to me. It is a moment I will forever treasure, and it captures the spirit of a truly remarkable young man who touched us all.
